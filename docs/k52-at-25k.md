### 1.1 Why no knob closes a 3.5 % gap to a mature encoder

K 52 at 25,000 B, holding **AVM's own** JOD 7.696 and BA 12.68, needs
**SS2 = +2.42**. AVM has SS2 -3.19 at that rate — short by 5.6 points — and its
SS2 slope above 32 kB is only 0.94 points/kB, so AVM itself reaches +2.42 only
at ~27,009 B. Reproducing AVM's *metrics* at 25 kB is therefore already beyond
AVM; beating them by ~1 K is not something a preset sweep can reach.

### 3.2 The key residual path is already tuned (coder-level attempt)

The reviewer asked for an actual coder change rather than a preset sweep, on the
measured defect (we keep ~1.8x AVM's high-frequency energy while correlating
worse, 0.51 vs 0.55). Every reachable knob on the key residual path was swept at
25,010 B and **none beats the baseline**:

| key-path arm | K @25,010 | vs 47.82 |
|---|---:|---:|
| reference | **47.82** | — |
| `EFF_PERC_KEY_CSF` 3 (more low-band weight) | 47.71 | -0.11 |
| `RD_KEY_LEVEL=0` (no key trellis) | 47.43 | -0.39 |
| `RD_KEY_LEVEL_SCALE` 0.001 / 0.05 / 0.01 | 47.83 / 47.38 / 47.8 | within 0.05 or worse |
| `EFF_PERC_KEY_CSF` 1 / 0.5 / 0 | ≤47.07 | negative |
| `RD_KEY_TX_TOP=8` | 47.82 | identical |

**New gate added, measured null: `AV2ENC_RDOQ_ZERO_TO_ONE`.** The scalar
quantiser is `lround(coeff * quant_factor)`, so anything under half a step is
hard-zeroed and the trellis can only refine survivors — a plausible mechanism for
detail that is present but misplaced. Wiring
`rdoq::Config::allow_zero_to_one` (default off, pins byte-identical) changes the
stream at **none** of QP 180/184/186/190/200: the trellis correctly declines
those levels because they cost more bits than the distortion they remove. This
confirms `rdoq.h`'s "those levels are normally dominated" rather than refuting
it. Kept env-gated for future QP regimes.

### 3.3 Content-adaptive per-superblock allocation: implemented, measured null

The reviewer's next step was a genuine bit-allocation change rather than a knob:
spend rate where SSIMULACRA2 charges (the 1-px band, `docs/band-d.md`: local SS2
tracks 1-px error at r = -0.82).

**Implemented** `AV2ENC_RD_DRIVER_SBALLOC` in `video_frame_decision.cpp`: the key
driver's tree lambda (both sites — the leaf cost and the tree prune) is scaled per
superblock by `(act_sb / act_frame) ^ -strength`, where `act` is
`seq_source_activity`, the same (d - box3x3(d))^2 1-px high-pass energy the BA
leaf D uses. `AV2ENC_RD_DRIVER_SBALLOC_MAX` caps the scale (default 4x). The
strength read is **signed** so the opposite direction is reachable as a control.
Unset or `0` is exactly 1.0 everywhere and byte-identical.

It does allocate — the stream changes at every non-zero strength:

| strength | bytes @QP186 |
|---:|---:|
| 0 (ref) | 25,172 |
| +0.25 | 25,070 |
| +0.5 | 24,876 |
| +1.0 | 24,813 |
| -0.5 | 25,318 |

**Measured at 25,010 B, both signs, five QPs each:**

| strength | K @25,010 | vs ref 47.82 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 (ref) | **47.82** | — |
| +0.25 | 47.69 | -0.13 |
| +1.0 | 47.19 | -0.63 |
| +0.5 | 47.08 | -0.74 |

| -0.5 | 47.64 | -0.18 |

Both directions lose, and the loss grows monotonically with |strength|. So the

allocation is already proportionate to where the error sits (62.8 % of bits on
63.7 % of the error): there is no mis-allocation left to correct, which is why
every allocator-shaped arm measured inert or negative.

The gate is kept env-only and default-off (pins byte-identical); it is a usable
negative for future QP regimes rather than a shipped lever.

### 3.4 Error decomposition: the deficit is luma magnitude, not shape or chroma

`tools/hk_diag.py` (added this wave) decomposes a decoded stream's error by
plane, by nested frequency band, and by source activity. Measured at **equal
rate** (ours QP187 24,240 B against AVM QP171 24,204 B):

| | ours QP187 | AVM QP171 |
|---|---:|---:|
| luma rmse | **15.36** | **13.31** |
| luma error share of total | 78.8 % | 73.8 % |
| 1-px band share of luma error | 78.3 % | 81.8 % |
| HF/mid energy ratio | 5.30 | 6.28 |
| textured / mid / flat 16-px rmse | 20.4 / 15.3 / 7.3 | 17.4 / 13.5 / 6.6 |
| corr(our error, AVM error) | 0.62 | — |

Three findings, and they **correct** the "detail present but misplaced" reading
in §3.2:

1. **The error *shape* is essentially AVM's.** The 1-px band carries ~80 % of
   luma error in both. That concentration is intrinsic to this content at this
   rate, not a defect — the source carries only 33 % of its energy there, but
   rate-starved coding always errs at the finest scale.
2. **Our error is uniformly larger**, by 15 % rmse (58.7 MSE), and we are if
   anything *less* high-frequency weighted (HF/mid 5.30 against AVM's 6.28). A
   uniformly scaled error cannot be fixed by any spectral/band weighting — which
   is the mechanism behind every failed band-D, CSF and allocator arm.
3. **The deficit is luma, not chroma.** Swapping in the source's chroma plane
   moves SS2 from -12.41 to only **+0.46**; swapping in the source's luma moves
   it to **+58.0**. Chroma is 21 % of error energy and is already competitive
   (our U rmse 6.91 against AVM's 6.99 at equal rate). Chroma spend cannot close
   this, consistent with §3's chroma arms.

So the remaining gap is **quantiser accuracy at fixed bits on luma**: the same
bytes produce 15 % more error. That is a coefficient-domain/efficiency problem,
not allocation, not chroma, not spectral shaping.

### 3.5 Coefficient-domain accuracy levers: all measured at or below baseline

Following §3.4 (deficit is uniformly scaled luma error at equal rate), the
coefficient-domain accuracy knobs were measured at 25,010 B:

| arm | K @25,010 | vs 47.82 |
|---|---:|---:|
| reference | **47.82** | — |
| `AV2ENC_RDOQ_PREV_CAP` 3 / 4 / 6 / 9 | 47.82 | **byte-identical** |
| `--video-rdoq-scale` 0.015 | 47.77 | -0.05 |
| `--video-rdoq-scale` 0.004 | 47.62 | -0.20 |
| `--video-rdoq-scale` 0.008 | 47.41 | -0.41 |
| `--video-rdoq-scale` 0.030 / 0.060 | ≤48.2 @QP185 | no gain at equal bytes |

**`prev_level_cap` is inert by construction, not by tuning.** `rdoq.cpp`'s
`level_bound_for()` computes `raw = floor(2*ratio) + 1` and then
`value = max(raw, prev_level_cap)` — the cap only *widens the loop bound*, and
the DP's distortion stop rule terminates the search before that bound is ever
reached at this QP. So the trellis is not truncated; raising the cap cannot find
more candidates. This also explains why the `AV2ENC_RDOQ_ZERO_TO_ONE` gate
measured byte-identical (§3.2): the same stop rule, not the state space, governs.

**The trellis rate model is already at its optimum.** The default
`rdoq_sse_scale` the QP-scaled fit resolves to at this step (~0.016-0.021) sits
between the two worse arms (0.008 and 0.004); both cheaper and dearer pricing
lose. The trellis is therefore choosing correct levels for its model — the
remaining 15 % luma-rmse deficit is in the *model's fidelity to the live §8.3
cost* (`video_tx_search.cpp` prices with the static tables at
`txb_skip_ctx = dc_sign_ctx = 0`, documented as the "same size as the coding
block" estimate), not in the search over levels.

### 3.6 Live §8.3.2 contexts in the TX search: implemented, +0.03 K

The reviewer's identified defect: the TX search priced candidates with the
static §8.3 tables at fixed `txb_skip_ctx = 0`, `dc_sign_ctx = 0`. Implemented
`AV2ENC_TX_LIVE_CTX` (default off):

* a per-4x4 luma above/left grid of "any non-zero coefficient" and "DC non-zero"
  states, committed from each coded leaf (`live_ctx_commit`);
* `live_ctx_for()` deriving the real §8.3.2 contexts —
  `(min(above_or,4) + min(left_or,4) + 3) >> 1`, with context 0 when the part
  covers the whole leaf (per §8.3.2.2), and DC-sign from the above/left DC
  categories;
* threaded through `search_luma_tx` into both pricing sites: the
  `block_syntax_bits` scalar path **and** the RDOQ trellis `BlockInfo` (which had
  its own hardcoded zeros and would otherwise have ignored the contexts).

**First measurement bug, then a real one.** The first version passed `w/4, h/4`
for both leaf and transform size, so the §8.3.2.2 equality always fired:
instrumentation showed `10000 blocks, 0 nonzero txb_skip`. The key-TX log
explains why that was wrong — 2,323 leaves produce **6,089 transform parts**
(`none=383 split=913 horz=513 vert=514`), so most parts are sub-leaf. Deriving
the context **per part** (from `split.mi_row/mi_col` and `split.tx_size`) gives
`197000 blocks, 165894 nonzero txb_skip`.

| arm | K @25,010 B | note |
|---|---:|---|
| reference | **47.82** | |
| `AV2ENC_TX_LIVE_CTX=1` | **47.85** | +0.03 — real direction, inside the ripple |
| `+ --video-rdoq-scale 0.015` | 47.59 | negative |

At equal **QP** it is consistently better (QP186: 48.02 against 47.92; QP185:
48.31 against 48.26), which is the signature of a genuine small improvement
rather than noise — but at equal **bytes** it is only +0.03 K. `tools/kpi.py` on
the QP186 arm: `cvvdp=7.3420 butteraugli=12.7971 ssimulacra2=-10.644 K=48.02`;
`hk_eval` at 25,010 B gives 47.85, `validate_dav2d` exit 0.

**Verdict: correct fix, negligible size.** Priced-context accuracy was a real
defect and fixing it moves K the right way, but by 0.03 K rather than the 4.1 K
needed — so it is not the dominant term. Kept env-gated and default-off; all
three pins byte-identical with the gate unset.

### 3.6b "Larger transforms are unreachable": not true, and forcing them loses

The key-TX histogram looks like a defect (`tx32=20, tx64=0` of 6,089 parts), so
the obvious structural guess is that the search never considers a large
transform. `AV2ENC_RD_KEY_TX_FORCE_NONE` (default off) settles it by restricting
the candidate set to the largest-transform option:

```
baseline    plans[none=383 split=913 horz=513 vert=514]   25,172 B @QP186
force-none  plans[none=2562 split=0 horz=0 vert=0]        22,551 B @QP186
```

Two facts follow. First, `kNone` is **always** a candidate (`options.push_back`
unconditionally, §9.2 permits 64x64 for mi_size 13-18), so "make larger
transforms reachable" was already true — the search tries them and rejects them
on RD for 83% of leaves. Second, when it is forced the stream shrinks 10% at
fixed QP, which looks like a win until it is priced at equal bytes:

| arm | K @25,010 | vs live 47.86 |
|---|---:|---:|
| force-none | **46.70** | **-1.16** |

(Bracket 24,779-25,790 B; points QP182-186 = 47.31/47.37/46.50/46.00/45.46.)

So the TX-partition search is pricing the larger transform correctly: one 32x32
or 64x64 transform cannot represent this content's detail at this rate, and the
saved syntax buys back less quality than it costs. This closes the whole
"large-transform" branch of the residual-representation redesign as a measured
negative rather than a hypothesis.

### 3.6c Live CDF rows in key pricing: structurally inert on a key picture

The last open branch of the redesign was to stop pricing the key luma syntax
with the static §9.3 tables and use the CDF rows the tile writer actually starts
from. The key path passed `cdf_slice = nullptr`; the inter path already had the
option behind `AV2ENC_PIPE_BA`. Added `AV2ENC_RD_KEY_LIVE_CDF` (default off)
wiring `eff_cdf_slice()` — the frame-start §8.2 image — into the key candidate
pricing, and rebuilt. All three pins stay byte-identical with it unset.

**It is byte-identical with it set, too** — at every point tested:

| stream | gate off | gate on |
|---|---|---|
| 720p still QP186 | 25,172 B `8f0b85d8` | 25,172 B `8f0b85d8` |
| 720p still QP187 | 24,886 B `6e0f1c9c` | 24,886 B `6e0f1c9c` |
| chained 60 f QP121 gops 7 | 23,066 B `72e04413` | 23,066 B `72e04413` |

The reason is structural, not a wiring bug. `ensure_rate_interval_cdfs()` builds
the store from `cdf_seed_`, and `load_store_from_image()` falls back to
`load_defaults(out, base_q_idx)` when that image is empty. On a **key** frame the
seed is not carried state: `video_frame.cpp` derives it with
`cdf_image_defaults_for_base_q_idx(cdf_base_q_idx)` — the §9.3 defaults for that
frame's own `base_q_idx`, which is exactly what the static slice already prices.
A key picture is the first frame, so there is no adapted state for it to read;
the 60-frame chain is identical for the same reason (the gate only reaches the
key path, and the key is still frame 0).

So "price the key with the live CDF rows" has no content on a key picture. The
only way it could differ is on a key inserted *after* coding has started (drift
refresh), which is not the 25 KB case. This branch is closed by construction
rather than by measurement.

### 3.6d The 1-px checkerboard: real, ours is heavier, and it costs no K

Two user observations drove this wave: "the checkerboard doesn't really appear in
AVM but it does in av2enc", and "the quantiser keeps high-frequency detail when
it shouldn't be more than lower frequency".

**Measurement.** Projecting each 8x8 block onto the 1-px alternating basis over
blocks whose *source* has no such pattern (|amp| < 0.5, 71.4% of blocks):

| | ours | AVM |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean \|amp\|, flat blocks | **0.1498** | 0.1086 |
| signed mean | +0.0007 | −0.0019 |
| flat blocks over 0.5 | **4.9%** | 1.6% |
| max amp, whole frame | 3.52 | 1.06 |

So the observation is **correct**: our spurious checkerboard is 1.38x AVM's and
six times more often visible. The signed mean being ~0 shows it is unbiased
noise, not a systematic pattern — a quantiser-decision effect, not a bias.

**It is not the deficit.** Removing the entire alternating basis from our best
decode and re-scoring:

| alternating basis removed | K |
|---:|---:|
| 0% | 47.37 |
| 50% | 47.37 |

Zero K. The artifact is visually conspicuous but perceptually free, so it cannot
be the 4 K gap — which is consistent with §3.4: our *error* is uniformly ~15%

false.** Retained energy relative to the source, per band:

| band | ours | AVM |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 px | 0.455 | 0.318 |

the optimum rather than over-retaining:

| perturbation | K |
|---|---:|
| remove 25% of 1-px band | 47.46 |
| no change | **47.37** |
| remove 50% / 100% | 46.58 / 44.55 |

Any movement of energy between bands loses. A band-redistribution knob — what
`AV2ENC_EFF_PERC_KEY_CSF` and the dead-zone families already tried — cannot
close the gap; only a *better reconstruction at the same bits* can.

### 3.6e Two rejected attempts (kept here so they are not repeated)

**Single-picture delta-Q (reverted, `bcbf318`).** Treating `frames.size() == 1`
as schedule-free made `--video-delta-q` and `--video-aq` encode (rc 0), but
`dav2d` rejects every such stream (`Error decoding frame: Invalid argument. No
data decoded`) at QP176/180/186 while the same flags without delta-Q decode rc 0
and the pinned best stream decodes rc 0. The schedule guard protects a real
§5.18.7 header-writer limitation, not preset noise, so the edge-path precedent
does not transfer. Both guards are restored; delta-Q is rejected again (rc 2).

**Rate-aware intra mode choice (kept, flat).** `search_luma` compared raw SSE
with no rate term while the mode family costs 13.9% of the stream (27,894 of
200,158 information bits for 14,136 symbols at QP186), so the search ignored the
bits it was spending. `AV2ENC_RD_MODE_RD` adds `lambda * (bits - bits_DC)` at the
point of choice. At 25,010 B: 2 → 47.90, 8 → 47.90, 32 → 47.97, 128 → 47.54,
against ref 47.82. Inside the +0.15 gate, so the mode search is not where the
deficit lives either. Default off; pins byte-identical.

### 3.6f Checkerboard: cause identified, and it cannot move K

Follow-up to §3.6d, with the mechanism pinned down.

**It is not the loop filters.** Turning them off makes it *worse*
(flat-block amplitude 0.160 filters-off against 0.150 all-on), so deblock/CDEF/
CCSO are partly suppressing it rather than creating it. It comes from the
transform/quantiser stage.

**It is not a fabricated pattern.** In the flat-source blocks the *source* itself
carries a mean 0.164 checkerboard amplitude; ours is 0.150 and AVM's 0.109. So
we are **tracking real 1-px content more closely than AVM**, not inventing a
pattern. The visible excess is 71% of blocks × mean +0.041 over AVM.

**It contributes 0.007% of the error.** In MSE terms it accounts for ~0.016 of
the frame's 236.0 luma MSE, which is why every removal experiment returns
exactly the same score:

| experiment | K |
|---|---:|
| remove 0% / 50% / 100% of the alternating basis (whole frame) | 47.37 / 47.37 / 47.37 |
| suppress 0% / 50% / 100% in flat-source blocks only | 47.37 / 47.37 / 47.37 |

SS2 moves by 0.003 between the extremes. So the artifact is visually
conspicuous and perceptually free: **it cannot be the 4 K gap, and suppressing it
is not worth a single byte of the budget.** The real deficit remains the uniform
~15% reconstruction gap of §3.4.

### 3.6g 4x4 transforms are blocked by the leaf floor, not by pricing

`tx4=0` of 6,089 parts on the best arm is not a search decision. §9.2 gives
`Max_Tx_Size_Rect` = 8x8 for an 8x8 block and only `SPLIT` takes it to 4x4, so
4x4 is legal only from 8x8 coding blocks. The best arm pins the driver floor at
16x16 (`AV2ENC_RD_DRIVER_MIN=16`), so no 4x4 transform can exist. Lowering the
floor confirms it is reachable and that it costs what the floor-measurements
already showed:

| floor | plans | tx4 | tx8 | tx16 | bytes @QP186 |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | none=383 split=913 horz=513 vert=514 | 0 | 1,840 | 955 | 25,172 |
| 8 | none=1287 split=1061 horz=1255 vert=1008 | **996** | 2,227 | 652 | 28,265 |

### 3.6h The picture reads "darker": the loop filters crush highlights

User report: "av2enc looks darker than the source image". Measured at QP186
against the source, split by source luma:

| source luma | ours (filters on) | ours (filters off) | AVM (filters on) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 16-62 (shadows) | **+5.81** | +3.55 | — |
| 62-107 | +0.96 | +0.74 | — |
| 107-145 | -1.24 | -1.12 | — |
| 145-166 | **-3.00** | -2.20 | **-2.18** |
| 166-235 (highlights) | **-7.95** | -4.98 | — |

So the *global* mean is fine (ours 105.483 against the source's 105.432, i.e.
0.05 **brighter**, and AVM 105.384) — there is no brightness offset. What is
wrong is a **contrast stretch around mid-grey**: our reconstruction lifts
shadows by ~5.8 levels and crushes highlights by ~8.0, a spread of ~13.8 where
the source is flat. Turning the loop filters off halves it (3.5 / 5.0) and AVM
sits at -2.2 in the one band where it can be compared.

The consequence shows up in the gain fit `recon = a*src + b`: ours a = 0.912,
AVM a = 0.934. We lose 8.8% of contrast where AVM loses 6.6% — a third more.
Per octile, the loss is worst in 145-166 (0.847 against AVM's 0.904) and the
highlights (0.797 against 0.813), and essentially perfect in the shadows (0.998).
That asymmetric, highlight-biased crush is what reads as "darker and muddier".

Removing the filters is not the fix: filters-off scores K 47.09 against 47.92, so
they earn their place. The darkening is *inside* the filter chain, not caused by
having one, and the correction has to be a retune rather than a removal.

### 3.6i High-frequency band mask: first arm that is strictly better

`AV2ENC_TX_HF_MASK_DIV` (default 0 = off) zeros the transform's outermost band
before quantisation, so 1-px detail costs no levels and the bytes are refunded.
Masking `(row >= w/div, col >= h/div)` with div=2 keeps the 8x8 (4..7, 4..7)
corner, where the checkerboard basis lives.

Confirmed with `hk_eval` at the 25,010 B target and `validate_dav2d` rc 0:

| arm | K @25,010 B | bytes @QP186 | vs ref (47.82) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ref | 47.82 | 25,172 | — |
| **hf-mask div=2** | **47.94** | **25,085** | **+0.12** |

Per QP it is better at four of five points (QP185 +0.28, QP186 +0.07, QP187
+0.05, QP189 +0.10) with QP188 an outlier at -0.80, so the direction is real
even though the size is small. Bracket gap 0.04.

**It is not a band-error reduction.** Measured 1-px error energy at QP186 is
129.17 for ref against 129.64 for div=2 — the finest band's error is unchanged.
The mask changes *which coefficients are coded* (and the partition decisions that
follow), not how much 1-px error the picture ends up with. Bytes freed are small,
mean 39 B per frame. So this is a decision-level improvement, not the band
surgery §3.6f supposed was impossible — the +0.12 K is real but the mechanism is
narrow, and it is 0.12 of the 4 K needed.

### 3.6j Where the "darker" look really comes from

Ablating the filter chain shows the crush is **not** one filter:

| arm | 16-62 | 145-166 | 166-235 | gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all filters | +5.81 | -3.00 | **-7.95** | 0.9136 |
| deblock off | +5.51 | -2.86 | -8.07 | 0.9157 |
| filters off | +3.55 | -2.20 | -4.98 | 0.9452 |
| AVM | +4.57 | -2.18 | -5.91 | 0.9337 |
| ccso-lambda 5 / 50 | +5.81 | -3.00 | -7.95 | — |

Three findings. Deblock is irrelevant to it. CCSO lambda is byte-inert (5 and 50
give the identical bias). And **filters-off still crushes highlights by -4.98**,
so most of the bias is upstream of the filters in the quantiser/reconstruction —
AVM carries a similar -5.91, so some crush is intrinsic to coding this content at
this rate.

What the filters *add* on top of our own unfiltered recon is a genuine contrast
stretch: **+2.26 in shadows and -2.97 in highlights**. That is the only place we
exceed AVM by a wide margin (delta -2.04 in highlights, against -0.05 to
+1.24 everywhere else). So the correct statement is: the picture is not darker
(mean is 0.05 brighter than the source) but **over-contrasted by the filter
chain**, which reads as crushed highlights and dirty shadows. Removing the
filters scores 47.09, so the fix has to be a retune, and no exposed knob
(deblock, ccso lambda) reaches it.

### 3.6k Bit census: where the 25 kB actually goes, and why eob is the target

`AV2ENC_BIT_CENSUS` gives the fine symbol split at QP186 (200,158 information

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| coeff_base | 122,547 | 85,937 | **42.9%** | 0.701 |
| **eob** | 25,619 | **36,364** | **18.2%** | 1.419 |
| coeff_mag | 28,686 | 28,686 | 14.3% | 1.000 |

**eob is 13.1% of the residual symbols but 18.2% of the bits**, and it is

**Two suspected defects there were checked and are not defects.** The model

estimate matches the coder. And the coefficient contexts are frozen from scalar
pre-levels, which `rdoq/README.md` documents as the standard RDOQ approximation
that keeps the DP additive — replacing it would break the trellis's optimality
rather than improve it. Neither is a bug to patch.

### 3.6l CDEF strength is not the contrast stretch

`AV2ENC_CDEF_MAX_STRENGTH` (default 0) caps the CDEF ladder's luma primary
strength by filtering the candidate pool before the search. Pins byte-identical
when unset, and **caps of 2, 4 and 6 are all byte-identical to the baseline**,
which proves the SSE-ranked search never selects a set above 2 on this picture.
So a strong CDEF is not what crushes the highlights, and bounding the ladder
cannot fix the look.

The chain's effect measured directly (filters-on minus filters-off, by source
luma) is a monotone pull toward the interior of the range — +2.30 in shadows,
-2.97 in highlights — touching 72-86% of pixels in every band. That is simply
what a low-pass/smoothing chain does at the extremes, and it is the chain as a
whole rather than any single stage (deblock irrelevant, CCSO lambda byte-inert,
CDEF capped at 2).

### 3.6m Exact-cost trellis: implemented, and it is worse

`Model::cost_symbol` routes to an installed `SymbolSink`, and `ExactSink`
(a real §8.2 range encoder per symbol, in `video_rate_interval.h`) already
existed but had **never been plugged into the coefficient trellis** —
`AV2ENC_RATE_INTERVAL` only reaches `plan_rate_bits`, so `interval` and `exact`
were both byte-inert there (verified: all three modes give 25,172 / `8f0b85d8`).

`AV2ENC_RDOQ_EXACT` installs the sink around `rdoq_quantize` (cleared
immediately after, so no later block inherits it). It bites: the plan histogram
moves from `381/913/513/514` to `374/904/507/534`, so the trellis really is
re-deciding under exact prices.

| arm | bytes @QP186 | K |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline | 25,172 | **47.92** |
| exact-cost trellis | 25,258 | 47.81 |

**Worse, and by more than the +86 B.** The reason is structural: `ExactSink` is
reset per block (it must be, or the estimate would depend on block order), which
throws away the frame's adapted CDF state that the real tile coder carries. So
it prices each block against a *fresh* CDF while the writer uses an *adapted*
one — a different inconsistency from the static model's, and a larger one. The
static `-log2(p)` model is the better estimator here. Default off; pins
byte-identical.

### 3.6o EOB density measured: already concentrated, so there is nothing to fix

The census (§3.6k) showed eob at 18.2% of bits and prompted a scattered-EOB
hypothesis. `AV2ENC_KEY_EOB_HIST` (diagnostic, default off; verified) reports the
EOB position bucketed by the fraction of the transform the last non-zero
coefficient covers:

| QP | 0-12.5% | 12.5-25% | 25-37.5% | 37.5-50% | 50-62.5% | >62.5% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 186 | **54.5** | 24.6 | 13.5 | 5.5 | 1.7 | 0.2 / 0 / 0 |
| 187 | **56.8** | 24.4 | 12.1 | 5.1 | 1.3 | 0.3 / 0 / 0 |
| 189 | **61.3** | 23.6 | 10.7 | 3.5 | 0.8 | 0.1 / 0 / 0 |

Our blocks terminate **early** — 93% inside the first 37.5% of the scan at
QP186, and the concentration *rises* as rate falls. The trellis is not emitting
scattered EOBs, so "charge the future EOB cost of a late coefficient" has
nothing to act on and is not worth implementing.

The 1.42 bits/symbol is the **irreducible per-block termination signal**, and the
arithmetic confirms it: 25,619 blocks each paying ~1.42 bits to say "this is my
last coefficient" *is* the 36,364 bits, and with 54.5% of blocks at eob_pt 1-2
(1-2 symbols) that rate is exactly what the model predicts.

**The real overhead is block count, and it is already optimally priced.** A
transform block costs `txb_skip + eob` = 3.94 bits to open and close, and across
10,735 parts that is **42,282 bits = 21.1% of the whole stream**. Halving the
block count would free ~2,643 bytes — but forcing larger transforms measures
K 46.70 against 47.86, so the fine partitioning's quality gain exceeds the
2.6 kB of overhead it costs. That is the same conclusion as §3.6b from the other
direction, now with the bits accounted for.

### 3.6p A per-block DC/AC step is not signallable; the trellis step is

The requested lever — refine the quantiser step per transform block where the
residual is dense — **cannot be implemented as a coding change**. §5.20.9.2
carries `CurrentQIndex` only at **superblock** granularity, updated through the
delta-Q plan (`video_syntax_block.cpp` asserts
`dqp_.tile_qp.current_q_index == step.qindex_after` per superblock, and
`step.qindex_after` comes from the per-SB plan). There is no per-transform-block
Q field, so a finer block step would dequantise on the decoder side with the
superblock's step and the stream would not reconstruct — the same failure mode
that made `--video-delta-q` produce streams `dav2d` rejects (rc 1,
`No data decoded`) in §3.6e.

That closes the coding-side form. What *is* legal is separating the step the
**RD search assumes** from the step the bitstream **codes**: `rdoq::Config::
quant_step` only prices levels inside the trellis, and the emitted levels are
dequantised with the coded step regardless. `AV2ENC_RDOQ_TRELLIS_STEP_SCALE`
(default 1.0) multiplies that pricing step, so the trellis keeps more levels
(s > 1) or fewer (s < 1) while the reconstruction stays exact. Pins
byte-identical at 1.0; it bites (s=0.9 gives 25,249 B `6251d323`, s=1.1 gives
25,242 B `b7d3794d` against the 25,172 B pin).

Where the 59.1% of coefficient-value bits actually sits, from the census:

| field | bits | note |
|---|---:|---|
| coeff_base | 85,937 (42.9%) | entropy-coded, 0.70 b/sym |
| coeff_mag | 28,686 (14.3%) | **exactly 1.000 b/sym, i.e. raw literals** |
| sign | 3,571 (1.8%) | 1.001 b/sym, irreducible |
| coeff_br | 63 (0.0%) | only 42 symbols |

`coeff_mag` is the `RawScope` catch-all for `raw_bit` inside `code_tx_view`.
**Corrected in §3.6u**: it is the non-CDF *sign* bits, not escape literals — the
§5.20.7.28 escape never fires on this stream, and a sign is 1 bit by
definition, so this 14.3% is incompressible.

`AV2ENC_RDOQ_TRELLIS_STEP_SCALE` (§3.6p) measured at QP186 with `validate_dav2d`

### 3.6s Predictor selection: the SSE argmin is already the RD argmin

Prediction is the larger error term (pred_rmse 24.53 / 24.83 / 26.79 at
MIN=4 / 8 / 16, against recon_rmse 15.32 / 15.45 / 15.95), and the key luma mode
search picks by pure SSE with no rate term, so the hypothesis was that the SSE
argmin is not the leaf-RD argmin. Both halves of that were measured.

**A better predictor does exist by SSE.** `AV2ENC_MODE_TOPN` keeps the best 8
candidates per block; over 10,000 evaluations the gap between rank 1 and rank 2
averages **7.7-7.8% of best SSE** (mean best 534,308, mean gap 41,697) and
exceeds 5% on **43%** of blocks. That is far from noise.

**But re-scoring by actual leaf RD never changes the winner.** `AV2ENC_RD_PREDICTOR`
re-runs `compute_tx` on the top-N candidates' predictors and picks the minimum
`sse + lambda * plan.rate_bits`. Instrumented: **`blocks=10000 flips=0`** — the
SSE argmin won every block, and the stream is byte-identical to baseline at n=4
and n=8 (`pred_rmse=26.7936`, `recon_rmse=15.9489`).

The reason is structural, not incidental: a worse predictor produces a *larger*
residual, which costs more in **both** SSE and rate. The two objectives point the
same way, so their argmins coincide. The 7.8% SSE gap is always in the chosen
candidate's favour, and no re-scoring can exploit it. This is the "no better
predictor is selected because none prices better" outcome — the null is in the
*selection*, not in the candidate pool.

So the prediction deficit is a property of the **candidate set** (61 modes, one
transform family), not of the choice made from it. Closing it needs better
predictors, not better selection.

### 3.6t Prediction is not the binding term: MRL cuts pred_rmse 2.4-3.2% and loses K

§3.6s showed the SSE argmin is already the RD argmin, so the deficit had to be in
*which predictors exist*. The candidate set is the full AV2 luma set — 13 modes
with the complete ±3 × 3° angular sweep (`kMaxAngleDelta = 3`,
`kAngleStep = 3`), so no unimplemented mode could be added without changing
what is signalled. What the `--nightly-b` preset does **not** enable is MRL, so
that is an existing, signallable family outside the searched set, and it is the
direct test of whether better prediction buys K.

**MRL genuinely improves prediction, at every QP:**

| QP | ref pred_rmse | MRL pred_rmse | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 185 | 26.8124 | 26.1586 | **-2.44%** |
| 186 | 26.7936 | 26.1589 | **-2.37%** |
| 187 | 26.9001 | 26.1878 | **-2.65%** |
| 189 | 26.8979 | 26.0410 | **-3.19%** |

**And it loses K.** At QP186 MRL codes 25,488 B and scores K 47.72, where the
reference curve gives 48.07 at that rate — 0.35 K *below* the curve — while
`recon_rmse` actually rises (15.95 → 16.02). Confirmed on the earlier
independent screen: 47.43 against 47.82.

This is the discriminating outcome the plan called for, and it resolves
prediction's role: **a measurable pred_rmse reduction with no K gain means
prediction is NOT the binding term.** The MRL predictor is better in SSE but pays
for `mrl_index` signalling and leaves a residual the quantiser codes less
efficiently, so the "prediction is the larger error term" framing was the wrong
lever. The reconstruction deficit lives downstream of the predictor, in how the
residual is represented at this rate.

### 3.6u correction: `coeff_mag` is raw sign bits, not level magnitude

§3.6k and §3.6p both read the census's `coeff_mag` bucket as level-magnitude
data (escape literals). **That was wrong**, and the escape path does not even
execute on this stream.

**The §5.20.7.28 escape never fires.** Instrumented at the writer's
`write_quant_escape` entry: **0 calls** on the QP186 still. So no stream bits
come from the escape, and no "narrow the escape by better prediction" work can
save anything — there is nothing to narrow.

**The escape cannot be changed anyway.** Its predictor is normative:
`pred_level = hr_level_avg` with the update `hr_level_avg = (x + hr_level_avg)
>> 1` over the reverse scan, and the decoder reconstructs that state bit-exactly
from the same coded symbols. Altering `m`, `k`, `c_max` or the update rule would
make every escape-bearing stream undecodable — the delta-Q failure mode.
(The trellis *does* price it correctly: `escape_bits(x, p.hr_pred)` with
`p.hr_pred` built by the identical rule at `rdoq_av2_model.cpp:316-326`.)

**What `coeff_mag` actually is.** With the escape dead and `eob_extra` tagged
under `eob`, the only `raw_bit` left inside `code_tx_view` is the **non-CDF sign
bit** at `video_syntax_coeff.cpp:676`:

| bucket | raw_bit calls | bits |
|---|---:|---:|
| coeff_mag (non-CDF signs) | 28,686 | 28,686 |
| sign (CDF signs, luma DC) | 3,567 | 3,571 |
| total non-zero coefficients needing a sign | 32,253 | — |

A sign is 1.000 bit by definition, so **14.3% of the stream is incompressible
sign data** and the luma-DC case already uses a CDF. This removes `coeff_mag`
from the target list entirely.

### 3.6v Adaptive coefficient CDF in the trellis: mechanism works, K is flat

The census put 42.9% of bits in `coeff_base` at 0.70 b/sym under the **static**
§9.3 rows, and §3.6m showed the `ExactSink` arm lost accuracy by resetting per
block. `AV2ENC_RDOQ_ADAPT` is the missing combination: **one CDF bank for the
whole frame**, adapted exactly as the tile coder does (`av2::adapt_cdf`), priced
as ideal `-log2(p)` so the trellis DP stays additive.

**The premise is confirmed.** The static model over-prices the decided plans by
**6.48%** (170,946 estimated against 160,539 actually coded), i.e. it is
systematically pessimistic in exactly the direction the tile coder's own
adaptation moves the cost.

**The mechanism works.** Adaptation bites — the plan histogram moves
383/913/513/514 → 398/880/549/506 — and `coeff_base` improves from
**0.7013 to 0.6979 b/sym (-0.49%)** with `validate_dav2d` rc 0.

| arm | bytes @QP186 | K | ref @ bytes | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ref | 25,172 | 47.92 | 47.92 | 0.00 |
| adaptive CDF | 25,454 | 47.97 | 48.01 | **-0.04** |

**But K does not improve at matched bytes.** Total bits *rise* 200,158 →
202,342 (+1.1%) because the better per-symbol estimate changes the partition
shape: cheaper coeff_base symbols buy more, larger transforms, which spend more
on terminal and block-open/close. The 6.48% over-pricing was real but it was
biasing the *partition* decision, not costing coefficient bits, and correcting it
moves the layout to a worse operating point.

### 3.6w RD-engine calibration: not stale, and my over-correction hypothesis was wrong

The engine's static->live per-family scales are fitted at QP119/240p
(`txb 0.791, eob 0.893, coeff_base 0.921, coeff_br 0.937`) and we encode at
QP186/720p, so they looked like a stale-parameter defect worth refitting.

**Turning the calibration off is consistently smaller** — 0.25-0.43% at every
QP (25,110 / 24,171 / 23,476 / 22,402 / 26,078 at QP186/187/188/189/185 against
25,172 / 24,240 / 23,535 / 22,499 / 26,180). But it **loses K**: 47.70 against
47.92 at QP186 with Butteraugli 13.74 against 12.99, so the saved bytes are paid
for in the perceptual term. The calibration earns its place.

**Refitting does not help either, and both directions are worse.** Measured the
over-all model ratio at QP186: the trellis estimate is 170,946 bits against
160,539 actually coded, i.e. **1.0648x over-pricing**, which the defaults correct
by ~7.1% on the total (a 9.29% cut across the four scaled families, which are
~76% of the estimate). I reasoned this over-corrected slightly and that raising
the scales would shrink the stream. It does not:

| uniform multiplier on the four scales | bytes @QP186 |
|---|---:|
| **0.90** | 25,261 |
| **0.95** | 25,206 |
| **1.00 (default)** | **25,172** |
| **1.02** | 25,194 |
| **1.05** | 25,178 |

1.00 is a **local minimum in bytes** in both directions, so the QP119 fit happens
to be well-placed for QP186 despite the 67-QP extrapolation. The byte-cost of the
calibration and its K-benefit are two different things on two different metrics,
and the "stale parameter" reading of the 0.25-0.43% saving was wrong.

### 3.6x Per-family calibration scales and lambda: both closed

**Per-family scales** (from the completed screen, at 25,010 B against cal1's
47.82): `cal0` 47.63 (-0.19), `eo985` 47.30 (-0.52). Measured directly beyond the
screen: `bs95` 25,178 B, `tx95` 25,134 B but K **47.49** where the curve gives
47.90 (-0.41). So no single family or combination improves on the QP119 fit —
consistent with §3.6w, where a uniform multiplier was a byte minimum at 1.00.

**The RD lambda has a real bit-efficiency gradient, but it is the wrong trade.**
Since the model over-prices bits by 1.0648x (§3.6w), the "corrected" lambda should
be 1/1.0648 = 0.939x. Measured, lowering it makes streams *bigger*
(240 -> 25,280 B, 230 -> 25,376 B, 214 -> 25,426 B), i.e. the gradient runs the
other way. Raising it does shrink the stream:

| RD_DRIVER_LAMBDA | bytes @QP186 | K | ref curve @ bytes | delta |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 272 | 25,091 | 47.54 | 47.87 | -0.33 |
| 290 | 25,019 | 47.71 | 47.83 | -0.12 |
| 256 (default) | 25,172 | 47.92 | 47.92 | 0.00 |

The trellis scale shows the same shape (0.012 25,138 B, 0.016 25,276 B, 0.020
25,151 B, 0.024 25,182 B). So lambda 256 sits at a local byte minimum with the
lowest lambda going *up*, and every arm that does spend fewer bytes pays for it
in K below the curve. Lambda is therefore tuned, not stale, exactly like the
calibration it multiplies.

### 3.6y Coefficient contexts refined from the trellis's own levels: worse

The one coefficient-model change that is not a re-parameterisation: the contexts
are frozen from the **scalar** quantisation (`rdoq/README.md`'s documented
approximation), but the trellis then picks different levels, so every candidate
after the first is priced against a neighbourhood no code path ever emits.
`AV2ENC_RDOQ_CTX_REFINE` runs the trellis once, rewrites the scalar seeds with its
**own** chosen levels, rebuilds `build_positions` from those, and re-runs.

Verified first that the geometry is sound: the §5.20.7.30 diagonal walk puts every
below/right neighbour at a *higher* scan index (0 violations on 4x4), so
`kSigRef` points at genuinely already-coded coefficients and the neighbour
direction was never the problem — only the frozen values were.

It bites substantially — plans move 383/913/513/514 → 413/907/519/465 — and
`validate_dav2d` passes, but it is worse:

| arm | bytes @QP186 | K | ref curve @ bytes | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ref | 25,172 | 47.92 | 47.92 | 0.00 |
| context refine | 25,221 | 47.57 | 47.95 | **-0.38** |

So the frozen-context approximation is not costing anything: pricing candidates
in a self-consistent neighbourhood makes the *estimate* more accurate but changes
the partition to a worse operating point, which is the same pattern as §3.6v
(adaptive CDF) and §3.6x (lambda). Three independent attempts to make the rate
estimate more truthful all lose K, which says the estimator's bias is
**compensating** for something else rather than being a defect in its own right.

### 3.6z Per-family calibration family formally closed

Completed screen at 25,010 B against cal1's 47.82: `tx95` **47.84 (+0.02, noise)**,
`cal0` 47.63 (-0.19), `eo97` 47.63 (-0.19), `bs95` 47.41 (-0.41), `eo985` 47.30
(-0.52), `eo97bs95` did not bracket. No arm beats the QP119 fit.

### 3.6aa The contrast loss is beneficial, not a defect

§3.6h measured our gain at a = 0.912 against AVM's 0.934 and flagged it as the
one remaining structural difference. It is not a defect. Measured before
implementing any filter bias, by rescaling our own decode back toward full
contrast and re-scoring:

| contrast restored | gain | K | Butteraugli |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 0.9136 | **47.92** | **12.99** |
| 25% | 0.9354 | 47.74 | 13.78 |
| 50% | 0.9568 | 47.48 | 14.66 |
| 100% | 1.0000 | 46.68 | 17.08 |

K falls monotonically and Butteraugli carries almost all of it. The reason shows
up in the error: restoring contrast moves rmse 15.18 -> 18.33 while BA goes
12.99 -> 17.08, so Butteraugli penalises contrast *expansion* far more steeply
than SSE does. Our slight compression is the perceptual metric's preferred
operating point at this rate, and AVM's 0.934 comes from a **different
reconstruction**, not from its filters biasing ours.

So a deblock/CCSO bias at the filter output cannot help: driving our gain toward
AVM's number by rescaling makes K worse, and the only legal signalling channel
(CCSO `ccso_offset_idx`) would buy exactly that. This closes the filter-bias
family without implementing it.

### 3.6ab IDTX is reachable but never wins; the sub-8 guard is stale but inert

**IDTX is searched and loses.** `--video-idtx` is a signallable, opt-in TX family
and `--nightly-b` leaves it off, so `idtx=0` of 6,089 parts looked like an
unreachable capability. Enabled, it is selected **once** in 6,089 parts
(`dct=1661 adst=4430 idtx=1` against `1664/4425/0`) at 25,190 B against 25,172 —
so the search does reach it and the identity transform simply never pays on this
picture. The TX-type set is not deficient.

**The sub-8 IDTX guard is stale.** `video_frame.cpp` rejects
`--video-min-block 4` + `--video-idtx` with "the key+P chain is not decoder-exact
at the sub-8 search depths", citing a pinned-`dav2d` failure. Re-measured on the
current tree, **both `dav2d` (rc 0) and `avmdec` (1,382,400 B, full frame) decode
the idtx streams at MIN=8 and MIN=16**, and the guarded pair now encodes and
decodes (`rc 0`, 25,190 B). `AV2ENC_ALLOW_IDTX_SUB8` re-enables the pair for
measurement; unset keeps the historical guard and every pin byte-identical.

**But it buys nothing, because the guard was never the binding constraint.**
`--video-min-block 4` is **byte-identical** with and without it under the
`RD_DRIVER_MIN=16` floor (`fb871fc7…` both ways) — the driver's leaf floor, not
`min_block_size`, is what decides leaf size. So the blocked capability was inert
on this recipe, and its restoration changes no stream.

**On using `avmdec` when `dav2d` cannot decode:** they reconstruct byte-identically
(0 differing bytes on every stream compared, §3.4-era), so `avmdec` is a genuine
independent check that *confirms* the idtx streams are conformant rather than
papering over a real defect — which is what the measurement above relied on.

### 3.6ac Component combinations: none compose into a gain

Each component was measured alone against a baseline lacking the others, so the
combinations were untested. All at QP186, MIN=16:

| arm | bytes | K @25,010 | vs hf2 (47.94) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ref | 25,172 | 47.82 | -0.12 |
| **hf2 alone** | 25,085 | **47.94** | — |
| hf2 + idtx | **25,060** | 47.91 | **-0.03** |
| hf2 + mrl | 25,639 | — (more bytes, MRL's cost) | worse |
| hf2 + idtx + mrl | 25,673 | — | worse |
| idtx alone | 25,190 | — | worse |
| mrl alone | 25,488 | 47.72 | worse |

`hf2 + idtx` is the **smallest stream measured in this document** (25,060 B, and
smaller than ref at four of five QPs: -97/-112/-91/-120/-(-60)), and both `dav2d`
and `avmdec` decode it (rc 0). But at matched bytes it is **47.91 against hf2's

**TX type is not ADST-locked on 1-px-heavy leaves.** The per-part distribution is
`dct=1664 adst=4425 idtx=0` of 6,089 — DCT is chosen on **27.3%** and ADST on

class is stuck on one family. The split is the RD outcome, not a missing
signallable choice. `--video-dct-only` does not exist as a flag, so this is

### 3.6ad hf2+idtx is not better at any QP, and QP184-185 move away from the budget

The suggestion was to sweep QP 184-185 with hf2+idtx so the mask's saved bytes are
spent on quality. **Those QPs are finer than 186, so they produce more bytes, not
fewer** — QP184 gives 27,331 B for hf2+idtx against the 25,010 B target, i.e.

Measured across the curve, at matched points rather than interpolated:

|---|---:|---:|

### 3.6ae Decomposition at matched rate: the gap is a predictor gap, not a quantiser gap

The reviewer's decomposition — separate "our residual is larger going in" (a) from
"we discard more of the same-sized residual" (b→c) — is measurable with
`AV2ENC_PRED_DUMP`, which reports exactly those three quantities. `pred_rmse` is
(a), `recon_rmse` is (b) and `residual_keeps` is the energy ratio (c).

**The two terms separate cleanly.** Holding the quantiser fixed and varying the
*predictor* (driver floor) moves (a) while (c) tracks it:

| floor | bytes | pred_rmse (a) | recon_rmse (b) | keeps (c) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MIN=4 | 28,281 | 24.5100 | 15.4995 | 63.2% |
| MIN=8 | 27,416 | 24.8437 | 15.6368 | 62.9% |
| MIN=16 | 24,240 | 26.9001 | 16.1492 | 60.0% |
| MIN=32 | 21,925 | 30.4502 | 16.8157 | 55.2% |

Holding the predictor fixed and varying the *quantiser* (QP) leaves (a) essentially
constant and swings (c):

| QP | bytes | pred_rmse (a) | recon_rmse (b) | keeps (c) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 180 | 31,913 | 26.9611 | 14.7366 | 54.7% |
| 187 | 24,240 | 26.9001 | 16.1492 | 60.0% |
| 196 | 17,596 | 26.8953 | 17.8325 | 66.3% |

So (a) is a pure predictor property and (c) is the quantiser's, exactly as the
plan assumed.

**Placing AVM on the same axes decides the question.** AVM at 24,204 B has
`recon_rmse` 13.3144 against our 16.1492 at 24,240 B. Two mutually exclusive
readings, and our own curve discriminates them:

*If AVM's predictor equals ours* (26.9001), it must reach keeps
(13.3144/26.9001)² = **24.5%**. Our keeps-vs-rate slope is −0.81 %/kB, so our
curve reaches 24.5% keeps at **~68 kB** — AVM would be a **2.8x** more efficient
coefficient coder at equal rate. No encoder is 3x better at coefficient coding,
and §3.6v/§3.6y already showed our estimator is at its own optimum.

*If AVM's discard ratio equals ours* (60.0%), its `pred_rmse` must be
13.3144/0.600 = **22.19** against our 26.90 — a **17.5% better predictor**. Our
own design space already spans that: MIN=4 gives 24.51, and 22.19 is only a
further 9.5% below the best floor we have.

**Conclusion: the 3.1 K gap is a prediction gap, not a quantiser gap.** It is
equivalent to ~3.1 kB of rate on our own curve, and the only explanation
consistent with realistic coding efficiency is a predictor ~17% better in SSE
than any point in our floor sweep. That redirects the work to intra prediction
quality — and it is consistent with §3.6t (MRL improves pred_rmse by 2.4-3.2% but
pays for its signalling) and §3.6s (the SSE argmin is already the RD argmin, so
the candidate *set* must improve, not the selection).

### 3.7 Distortion-weighted trellis objective: 1.0 is already optimal

The trellis minimises `D + lambda*R` with plain squared error, so it cannot
prefer an allocation that places the same total error somewhere cheaper.
`AV2ENC_RDOQ_DIST_WEIGHT` multiplies both distortion terms in `rdoq_quantize`
(the per-coefficient term and the all-zero suffix, so the two halves of the
reported cost stay on one scale). Default 1.0; all three pins byte-identical.

The weight bites (QP187: 0.8 → 25,224 B, 1.0 → 24,886 B, 1.5 → 24,774 B), and
measured at 25,010 B over five QPs per arm:

| arm | K@25,010 | vs ref |
|---|---:|---:|
| live (contexts, §3.6) | **47.86** | +0.03 |
| ref | 47.82 | — |
| weight 1.2 | 47.74 | -0.09 |
| weight 1.5 | 47.67 | -0.15 |
| live + weight 1.2 | 47.62 | -0.21 |
| weight 2.0 | 47.61 | -0.21 |
| weight 0.8 | 47.32 | -0.50 |

Both directions lose and the loss grows away from 1.0, so plain SSE is already
the correct objective: the error is not mis-placed *within* a block, which is
the only trade a distortion weight can make.

### 3.8 Key rate-model scale: measured ripple, not a lever

The key trellis prices with the static §9.3 tables while the tile coder adapts
its CDFs, and the inter path compensates with a measured 0.4. Made the key's
scale reachable (`AV2ENC_RD_KEY_MODEL_RATE`, default 1.0; all three pins
byte-identical).

At 25,010 B: `0.60`/`0.65`/`0.70` all **47.96**, `0.55` 47.94, `0.8` 47.78,
`0.4` 47.74, `1.2` 47.43. That 47.96 is the highest figure measured anywhere in
this document, and `hk_eval` reproduces it at 25,010 B with `validate_dav2d`
exit 0 — but it is **one discrete decision flip, not a calibrated lever**:
weights 0.55, 0.60, 0.65 and 0.70 produce the *identical* QP186 stream
(25,097 B / K 48.03), and at QP 187-188 every weight is byte-identical to ref.

Per QP against ref it is a wash: worse at QP184 (-0.15) and QP185 (-0.56),
identical at QP187, better only at QP186 (+0.11). Two regressions, one tie, one
win — it fails the +0.15 gate on any per-QP reading, and the gap to K 52 is 4 K,
not 0.14 K.

## 4. Six real encoder switches found and fixed

All three are the same class: an ablation switch that `apply_video_nightly`
(the `--fast-decode` expansion) honours and `apply_video_nightly_b` silently
ignored. Each made a whole family of arms measure a phantom "identical".

### 4.1 `--video-deblock-off` ignored under `--nightly-b`

`quality` RD preset sets `video_deblock = true`; `apply_video_nightly_b` never
read the off-switch. Before: byte-identical to baseline (24,240 B
`b59f64f7…`). After: 24,239 B `24b521ec…` (distinct). Measured effect
**K 47.21 vs 47.82** — deblocking earns its place, so this closes a lever
rather than opening one.

### 4.2 `--video-cdf-chain-off` ignored under `--nightly-b`

The CDF chain was set unconditionally. Before: byte-identical. After:
QP186 27,778 B vs 25,172 B (the reset image is 2.6 kB larger, as documented).

### 4.2b `--video-tile-flush-off` / `--video-ccso-header-off` ignored under `--nightly-b`

The header/overhead wave was assigned unconditionally. Both now honour their
off-switch: 25,174 B `577dc80f…` and 25,195 B `8231aa0a…` (both were
byte-identical before). Measured effect at 25,010 B: **K 47.82 / 47.81** — the
header wave is not a lever, but it is now measurable.

`--video-ref-signal-off` and `--video-tile-group-off` are deliberately **not**
wired: their compact forms are gated to the plain key+P chain, and enabling them
broke every `--nightly-b` encode ("gated on the plain key+P chain: the
B/TIP/frame-type wrappers build their own sequence header"). That is a real
composition limit, not a missing switch.

### 4.2c `--video-tx-search-off` ignored under `--nightly-b`

Never read by `apply_video_nightly_b`. Now honours the switch, mirroring
`apply_video_nightly` (it also drops `--video-key-intra-txpart`, which requires
TX search):

```
--video-tx-search-off   before: 25,172 B 8f0b85d8  (identical)
                        after : 21,600 B 19c6d335
```

Measured effect: it is a **different, worse encoder** — K 43.4-45.2 across
QP183-187 against the baseline's 47.8 at 25,010 B. The TX search is earning its
place; the mutation simply is not tuned, which is why the aggregate enables it.

`--video-rdoq-off` was already wired but is byte-identical on a lone key: RDOQ
is not reached there, so that ablation is inert by construction rather than by
a missing switch.

### 4.3 `--video-cdf-per-frame-qp` ignored under `--nightly-b`

Now mirrored exactly from `apply_video_nightly` (including the
`user_carry`/`user_reset` precedence). This unblocks the CDF-composition
constraint, but AQ/delta-Q still fail the frame-schedule check (§3).

## 5. What reaching K 52 at 25 KB would need

Not a flag. In descending order of evidence:

1. **A coefficient-accuracy fix at fixed rate.** SS2 charges the 1-px error
   band (`docs/band-d.md`: local SS2 tracks it at r = -0.82); we keep ~1.8x
   AVM's high-frequency energy while correlating worse (0.51 vs 0.55) — the
   detail is present but misplaced. `docs/band-d.md` already closed the
   two-parameter band-D / band1 routes as unpaying ("**Verdict: stop**").
2. **~19 kB of rate efficiency** at equal JOD and SS2.
3. **A relaxed budget:** the same arm reaches **K 52.00 at ~33,250 B** and
   K 50.20 at 29,602 B (`docs/k50-720p.md`).

## 6. Harness traps hit this session

1. **A broken accept-probe reported six rejected tools as "ACCEPTED".** It
   grepped a truncated log instead of checking the exit status. Always check
   `rc`, then screen.
2. **Screens must bracket the byte budget.** Early screens ran QP 178-184
   (29-37 kB) and say nothing about 25 kB; `EFF_PERC_CSF` flips sign between
   those ranges.
3. **`--video-gops 2` on a one-frame input is a different encoder, not a no-op.** It
   enables the chained-form tools (key-QP offset -37, partition probe, BAWP
   carrier) and costs 4x the rate at a given QP (94,786 B vs 25,172 at QP 186).
   Walked down to 25 kB it is K 46.91 — better than our arm only above ~30 kB.
4. **`min8` and the adaptive floor win at equal QP and lose at equal bytes.**
   At QP 186 `m8a32` scores 50.32 vs 47.92 — but at +10 kB; walked down to
   25 kB it collapses to 43.52. Only equal-byte comparison is meaningful.
5. **`screen.jsonl` reads can race the writer** and return empty; re-read.
6. **A single-QP "identical" is not proof of inertness** — three of the
   byte-identical results above were bugs, not inert knobs.
